About Me

I am an incoming Ph.D. student in Computer Science at the University of Utah, where I will be joining the group of Prof. Fuheng Zhao in the UtahDB lab. My research interests center on semantic operations and NL2SQL — integrating large language models into database systems through semantic operators such as NL2SQL translation, semantic filters, and reasoning joins, so that data becomes accessible through natural language interfaces.

Before graduate school, I worked under Prof. Gene Cooperman at Northeastern University (introduced to me by my mentor Prof. Alok Singh Gahlot), where I contributed to McMini, an open-source DPOR-based model checker for multithreaded programs. I also built autonomous robotics systems, developed a reliable RF-based remote monitoring link as a research trainee at DRDO, and led large student teams — mentoring 500+ students through the Embedded Systems & Robotics Club.

My academic trajectory has consistently led me towards reliability and fault tolerance in systems, and I am excited to explore research at the intersection of scalable data systems, semantics, and correctness.

Research

McMini: DPOR-based Model Checker

Under Prof. Gene Cooperman, Northeastern University

  • Researched livelock detection heuristics — detecting potential livelocks by looking for livelock-like cycles within a process's execution trace under a round-robin schedule.
  • Developed an LLVM instrumentation plugin to intercept accesses to global variables, wrapping them with runtime checks to detect non-deterministic reads and writes (data races).
  • Implemented command-line flags to make the software easier to use.

My work on model checking taught me how different CPU schedules produce non-deterministic behaviour in concurrent programs. I am excited to apply this systems mindset to building scalable, correct, and human-centered data systems.
