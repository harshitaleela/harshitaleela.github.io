Research

Livelock Detection Using Cycle Decomposition in a Model Checker

Harshita Lila · Aayushi Gautam · Gene Cooperman

Northeastern University · McMini

I developed a livelock-detection heuristic for McMini, an in-situ stateless model checker for concurrent programs. The approach targets executions in which threads continue to execute but collectively fail to make meaningful progress.

  • Developed a cycle-decomposition approach for identifying interacting groups of threads involved in livelock.
  • Used visible-object sharing, including mutexes, semaphores, condition variables, and shared variables, as a runtime approximation for thread interaction.
  • Designed the approach to analyze bounded execution suffixes rather than exhaustively enumerate schedules.
  • Produced shorter and more interpretable livelock witnesses.
  • Established soundness conditions under explicit assumptions and characterized limitations involving nondeterminism and progress annotations.

Model CheckingConcurrencyFormal VerificationLivelock

Technical details

In an in-situ stateless model checker such as McMini, analysis is performed using information available from the current execution branch rather than a globally cached state space. The approach therefore approximates potential thread interaction using visible-object sharing: two transitions are considered related when they access a common visible object such as a mutex, semaphore, condition variable, or shared variable. This provides a conservative approximation of potential interaction between threads without requiring exhaustive exploration of alternative schedules.

The algorithm decomposes threads into groups based on their observed interactions and searches each group for state recurrence within a bounded execution suffix. It avoids exhaustive enumeration of schedules and does not require cross-schedule state caching. The result is a compact livelock witness that isolates the threads and execution cycle associated with the suspected non-progress behavior.

About Me

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at the University of Utah. My path into computing began with a B.E. in Electronics and Computer Engineering at MBM University, Jodhpur, where I started with robotics, embedded systems, and hands-on engineering.

Over time my interests moved from physical systems toward software systems and the challenge of making concurrent programs behave correctly. This direction took me to research with Prof. Gene Cooperman and Aayushi Gautam at Northeastern University on McMini and livelock detection, where I worked on model checking, LLVM instrumentation, and systems-level development.

During my Ph.D., I am beginning to explore database systems and data management, carrying forward the systems-and-correctness perspective I developed through concurrency and model checking.
